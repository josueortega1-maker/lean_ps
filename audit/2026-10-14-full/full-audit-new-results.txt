1. Endpoint quota normalization
-------------------------------
The existing endpoint estimate is E[raw cost] <= q*theta + log(m). The actual
exposure is exactly raw exposure divided by q, not a differently normalized
random object. Nonempty Fin(m*q) forces both integer m and q positive, so
division preserves the inequality and gives theta + log(m)/q. The older endpoint
bound follows by q >= 1 and log(m) >= 0. This matches eq:finite-endpoint in the
manuscript. No dropped initial-choice term or factor of m/n was found.

2. Finite analytic envelope
--------------------------
For a finite nonnegative sum of ramps with nonnegative arrival times, value(0)=0.
Concavity of 1-exp(-theta*r), applied to each ramp, gives
value(1) <= cost(theta,1)/(1-exp(-theta)), for theta > 0. If cost <= theta+a,
the resulting ratio bound is valid even though a is not assumed positive in
this first theorem: the hypothesis itself constrains its value.

For a>0 set x=sqrt(2a)>0. Taylor's lower bound yields exp(x)>=1+x+a. Therefore
(1+x+a)*exp(-x)<=1 and x+a <= (1+x+a)*(1-exp(-x)). The denominator is positive,
giving (x+a)/(1-exp(-x)) <= 1+x+a. This independently re-derives exactly the
Lean argument. No optimization claim stronger than this explicit choice is
used. The a=0 case is excluded here and handled separately for the actual PS
theorem when m=1.

3. Actual finite quota bound
---------------------------
For m>1, a=log(m)/q>0. The positive-quota hypothesis supplies the actual-process
instance; the exposure/rank and cost theorems discharge all stochastic premises.
For m=1, expectedPS_one_type proves exact rank one, so the zero square-root
case is correct. The theorem is genuinely about average type ranks in the
uniform independent preference sample, not copy ranks or a free function.
The result 1+sqrt(2 log(m)/q)+log(m)/q is valid but weaker than the manuscript's
supply-identity bound. It must not be reported as that sharper bound or as a
proof of strict critical-regime PS/RP separation.

4. Affine rank/exposure relation
-------------------------------
At every genuine post-initial switch the previous type is absent, so the new
active count satisfies 1<=a<=m-1. Thus m/(a+1) <= (m-1)/a. The code obtains
this fact from freshness, not from an assumed restriction on possible histories.
Conditional time and exposure are constant on the same history fiber. Rank
jumps vanish on nonfresh queries and after time one. Their nonnegative weights
preserve the inequality.

The initial jump is exactly 1; its exposure is exactly 1/m. The correction
1/m in the affine jump bound is consequently necessary and correct:
1 = (m-1)*(1/m)+1/m. Summing the correction counts one term per agent and then
divides by n=m*q, leaving 1/m rather than n/m or q/m. Hence
E Rank <= (1-1/m)*f_q(1)+1/m = 1+(1-1/m)*(f_q(1)-1).
This is the manuscript's finite-market improvement. The generic affine-sum
lemma does not require A or B positive: the only multiplication of an
inequality is by the positive averaging factor 1/n. Its explicit jump
hypothesis is discharged in both public application theorems. The weaker
compatibility theorems remain valid. m=1 yields a zero slope and rank <=1;
the impossible-switch branch is correctly eliminated by the active-count fact.

5. High-quota limits
-------------------
Eventually positive m and q suffice for the lower bound 1 and the finite
quota upper bound. If log(m)/q ->0, continuity of sqrt and the squeeze theorem
give expectedPS ->1. No assumption m->infinity is silently introduced.
For fixed m>0 and q->infinity, const/log quota division has the required limit,
including m=1. For q/log(m)->infinity, taking reciprocals gives log(m)/q->0.
Lean's total division makes q/log(1)=0; the divergent-ratio theorem therefore
does not incorrectly cover a permanently single-type sequence. Single-type
and arbitrary mixed sequences are covered by the primary log(m)/q theorem
whenever its stated hypotheses hold. Zeros in finitely many early terms are
harmless. The only conclusions are PS convergence, never RP convergence or
threshold necessity.

6. Bounded-varying-quota limits
-----------------------------
The proof takes a finite intersection of eventual neighborhoods for fixed
q in {1,...,Q}. The resulting market-size threshold can depend on fixed Q and
the neighborhood but not the selected q. Composing with m(k)->infinity and
an eventually bounded positive q(k) proves the advertised uniform extension.
There is no interchange of a limit over unbounded quotas with market size.
Q=0 has an empty quota range; the generic-filter result is then vacuous unless
the filter is trivial, which is mathematically correct. Bottom filters need
no NeBot assumption for this statement. For nontrivial filters m->infinity
eventually makes logStar(m)>0; no unintended early zero-denominator assertion
is made. This extension depends on the existing fixed-quota lower theorem;
it does not independently re-prove that theorem's probabilistic foundations.
